01 / The brief

From a broad question to a specific opportunity.

I completed this project individually. The proposal focuses on P&G’s healthcare business and explores guided self-care kits through a concept called CareGuide.

The presentation moves through research signals, a key insight, the proposed experience, and an illustrative business model. This was an academic brief about P&G, not commissioned client work. The business assumptions and concept screens were prepared for class discussion.

03 / Selected details

Build the argument through hierarchy and diagrams.

I used a consistent blue-and-white palette, large research figures, and repeated icon treatments to connect the slides. The journey diagram on slide 4 makes the decision step the visual focus.

The sequence moves from evidence to a decision point, then the proposed service and its business assumptions. The figures illustrate a student proposal; they are not measured business results or a validated healthcare service.

Academic concept. CareGuide is presented as a student proposal. Research sources appear in slide 12 of the class deck.

04 / Delivery and reflection

A complete proposal, with assumptions visible.

I completed the research, concept development, and presentation design individually. The final deliverable is a 12-slide Canva deck, including a reference slide.

A further revision would give each statistic a source on its own slide, reduce small supporting text, and separate forecast assumptions from evidence more explicitly. Those refinements matter when the deck must be read without a presenter.

Read the presentation outline
  1. Brief. Explore how P&G could grow over five years.
  2. Research. Introduce wellness priorities, P&G healthcare growth, and the subscription market. Sources are collected on slide 12.
  3. Insight. Access to information and products does not necessarily resolve uncertainty about the next step.
  4. Journey. Show the symptom, search, decide, treat, and recover sequence, with the decision step emphasized.
  5. Concept. Introduce CareGuide: a student proposal for self-care kits with guided information and refill support.
  6. Value. Group proposed benefits for households and for the business.
  7. Comparison. Compare the proposed experience with selected information and retail services. This is a class analysis, not a validated market audit.
  8. Experience. Illustrate a five-step journey with concept screens.
  9. Business model. Present memberships and kits. The illustrative calculation divides $4 million in fixed costs by a $76 contribution margin, giving about 52,632 subscribers; this is not a forecast or an actual result.
  10. Recommendation. Summarize why the proposal merits discussion.
  11. Close. Invite questions.
  12. References. List the sources used in the original class deck.
