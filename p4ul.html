An octopus prediction machine for a World Cup watch party.

During my internship at Manufactured, I prepared the cabinet artwork for print and designed parts for the coaster input, power supply, and servo string routing.

01 / The experience

How the machine was meant to work

The team designed a cabinet inspired by Paul the Octopus. A visitor would insert a coaster, watch Paul move, and receive a preprinted coaster with a prediction.

02 / Facade

Preparing the cabinet artwork for print

Manufactured provided the initial AI-generated concept. I recreated low-resolution elements as vectors in Illustrator, combined supplied and sourced artwork, and prepared the three facade panels for print. I also checked the window openings and mounting holes against the physical cabinet so the artwork lined up with the build.

03 / Fit and motion

Adjusting the parts to fit the build

The original model used the wrong coaster size, so I measured the coasters we had and updated the input in Fusion. I checked for collisions and clearance, then reprinted parts as needed. For the stainless-steel brackets, I used full-size paper templates to check the fit first.

This was my first time working with servos. I wired and labeled about 16 of them and helped test the tentacles. We started with one tentacle and tried different lengths and numbers of sections. In an early test, the taped connection between a servo and its string came loose.

04 / Build status

Where the build stood when I left

The facade was installed, and we had tested individual parts of the mechanism. The full motion and coaster sequence was still being worked on when my internship ended.
